About

My passion lies in researching and developing solutions that make cyberspace safer. The rapid advance of AI, machine learning, big data, and IoT brings challenges that demand new approaches — my aim is to build secure, scalable technologies that protect modern computing systems while enabling new possibilities.

My research centers on authentication, privacy, and security mechanisms in distributed systems. My dissertation, Origin-Bound Password Derivation for Phishing-Resistant Web Authentication, develops schemes that bind what a user types to where they type it — making credentials useless to an attacker's look-alike site, without asking a single server to change.

Before my PhD, I built mobile games in Unity3D, ran national youth programs in Jordan, engineered networks for a telecom regulator, and trained trainers for peace-building — a path that taught me technology matters most when real people can actually use it.

Research

Origin-Bound Password Derivation for Phishing-Resistant Web Authentication

PhD dissertation · Committee: Dr. Son Tran (chair), Dr. Bill Hamilton, Dr. Naveed Ul Mustafa, Dr. Don Fuqua (Dean's representative). The thesis develops client-only defenses that derive site-specific credentials bound to the genuine origin, neutralizing phishing and adversary-in-the-middle attacks while remaining deployable today.

AnchorPass — origin-bound password tokens

A client-only approach that transforms passwords into origin-bound tokens before they ever leave the browser. A phishing page receives a token bound to the wrong origin — worthless to replay. Requires zero server changes. Published at HCII 2026 (Springer LNCS).

Harbor — recoverable origin-bound passwords

Extends origin binding with recoverability: phishing- and AiTM-resistant authentication that survives lost devices, still with zero server changes. Published in JSSSEE 1(2), 2026.

Usable security — deniable encrypted messaging

Collaborative user study on how people understand deniability in encrypted messaging after hands-on social experience. Published at EuroUSEC '24 (ACM).

Earlier work — IoT authentication

MSc thesis at the University of Jordan: LUMDA-KERBEROS, a modified Kerberos protocol supporting secure multi-client, multi-server authentication for IoT deployments.
